Stage 1 · The Foundation

What happened?

Descriptive analytics summarizes raw historical data into meaningful insights — totals, averages, counts. It tells you the story of your past.

What is Descriptive Analytics?

It answers questions about past events using aggregated data — sums, averages, counts, percentages. Think of it as the rear-view mirror of business: clear, factual, and historical. It does not predict or advise.

Where is it used?

  • Monthly sales dashboards
  • Website traffic reports
  • HR headcount summaries
  • Inventory levels & KPIs

Stage 2 · The Detective

Why did it happen?

Diagnostic analytics drills into data to discover root causes — correlations, anomalies, and patterns that explain past results.

What is Diagnostic Analytics?

It goes beyond "what happened" to discover why it happened. Using drill-down, data discovery, correlation, and root-cause analysis, you pinpoint the drivers behind your metrics.

Where is it used?

  • Fraud detection alerts
  • Server incident root-cause
  • Churn analysis
  • A/B test result interpretation

Stage 3 · The Forecast

What will happen?

Predictive analytics uses historical patterns and statistical models to forecast future outcomes with a calculated confidence.

What is Predictive Analytics?

It applies statistical models, machine learning, and trend extrapolation to estimate future events. Predictions are probabilistic — not certainties — and improve with richer historical data.

Where is it used?

  • Sales & demand forecasting
  • Credit scoring
  • Customer churn prediction
  • Stock market & risk models

How to use this tool

  1. Step 1Edit the historical year-by-year base data in the table.
  2. Step 2Drag the growth-rate slider to model different scenarios.

Stage 4 · The Advisor

What should we do?

Prescriptive analytics recommends the optimal action by combining predictions, constraints, and business rules into a decision.

What is Prescriptive Analytics?

It is the most advanced form — it not only forecasts outcomes but also suggests the best decision given your goals, constraints, and predicted scenarios. It blends optimization, simulation, and business rules.

Where is it used?

  • Route & logistics optimization
  • Pricing & promotion planning
  • Supply-chain decisions
  • Resource scheduling

How to use this tool

  1. Step 1Edit the available route options (distance, toll, time).
  2. Step 2Choose your goal: fastest time or lowest cost.
  3. Step 3Read the recommended action and reasoning.
